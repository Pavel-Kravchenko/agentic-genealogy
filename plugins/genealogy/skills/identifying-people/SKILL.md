# Identifying people

## Core principle

A surname and a given name are not identifiers. In one village the same given name, patronymic and
surname recur within a generation, and the same village name recurs across provinces. Identity is proven
by a document that says it, or by several independent circumstances that agree; a match of name and
approximate age proves nothing.

## What counts, strongest first

| Evidence | Weight |
|---|---|
| A document that states the link directly (parents named in a baptism, "widow of ...") | Decides |
| Household composition: who lives with the person, which children, which ages | Strong |
| Parish, community of registration, confession | Strong |
| The hand of the same clerk writing the name in a series of entries | Strong for reading a name |
| Occupation and status words, read literally (farmhand is not farm owner) | Medium |
| Age stated in a form or a census | Weak by construction; a gap of several years between two documents is common |
| Same name, same village | Not evidence |
| Someone else's online tree | Working notes, not testimony |

Two women of the same name and close ages are separated by household, parish and confession, not by age.
Compare household, parish and confession before ages.

## Rules

1. **Unproven identity stays as separate persons.** Enter only what the new document says, as its own
   person. Link it to the candidate with a low-confidence "possibly the same / possibly related" link. Do
   not merge until a new document or the family decides. Do not touch the dates of the existing person.
   People who appear only in the new document (a child, a spouse) are attached to the new person, not to
   the candidate. Record an age as the document states it ("aged 40 in 1897"); a birth year computed from an
   age is a note, not a date.
2. **Never smooth a disputed date.** When a document and a gravestone, or two documents, disagree, keep
   both values with their sources in a dispute field. Only a new document resolves it.
3. **Kinship stated by a living relative is recorded as stated.** Do not recompute it from the graph and
   do not "correct" it: the graph may be largely an import of someone else's tree and is a party to
   the dispute, not the judge. If a calculation is needed, ask one plain question.
4. **Retellings are checked against images.** What a relative or correspondent says a document contains
   is a lead; the image of the document is the source.
5. **Someone else's tree may be your own.** Open its sources: if they point to a collaborative world tree
   or to a service you once uploaded to, it is your own data returning through third hands and confirms
   nothing. Look at the revision history: a surname changed several times with no source added is a
   note, not a finding. Match trees by the stable record identifier (`_UID`), never by name.
6. **A wrong reading of a surname, once found, means re-reading every document of that family** for the
   same name. Put the confirmed spelling into the name variants with its document; do not rename the person.
7. **Calendars and double dates.** A fixed gap (12 days in the 19th century, 13 from March 1900) is Old
   and New Style. Variable gaps in a Lutheran personal book (from none to weeks or months) are birth and baptism; take
   the first as the birth.
8. **A secondary source without imprint** (a clipping with no title and date) gives disputed dates only.
9. **In Latvian and Estonian material a "maiden name" in an online tree is often a farm name.** Check
   it against the family's farm in the revision lists before treating it as a surname.

## Namesake traps to rule out explicitly

- a full namesake in the same village (same given name, patronymic, surname), possibly with a spouse of
  the same name as yours;
- a village of the same name in another uezd or province (one village name can exist in several communes);
- a person who appears in an index under several spellings and is counted several times.

Write down which of these you ruled out and how.

## When you report an identification

State: the claim; the documents; which circumstances agree (household, parish, confession, clerk);
which namesakes were excluded; the confidence (high / medium / low); what would overturn it.

## Red flags

- "The age fits, so it is her"
- "Same name and village, merging"
- "The tree on the site confirms our date" (does it cite anything but us?)
- "Averaging the two dates" or "taking the more plausible one"
- "According to the graph he is actually her cousin, correcting what the relative said"
