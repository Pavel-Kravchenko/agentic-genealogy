# What a human does

Many steps in family research belong to a person from the start: registration, payment, a signature, documents of
kinship, a visit, and every contact between people. They are the normal route for those steps, not a fallback.
This file lists them as they came up (2026) and how an agent prepares each one so that the human spends minutes,
not hours. The examples are composed illustrations; the patterns come from real work, the particulars do not.

## The hand-over: what the agent prepares

For every step below the agent writes a short hand-over note and saves it to disk. It contains:

1. **The goal in one line**: which question this step answers, and what result would close it.
2. **The exact target**: site and page, archive and department, shelf mark (`fond <F>, opis <O>, delo <D>`), film
   and image numbers, or the person to write to. Never "search the archive for Primerov".
3. **The facts the human needs**, each with its source: names in every spelling that occurs (Primerov, Примеровъ,
   Primerow), dates, the place with its name and jurisdiction for the year (see `libraries-and-reference.md`).
4. **Two or three questions**, no more, in the order of importance.
5. **Turnaround and enclosures**: expected turnaround, documents to attach.
6. **The wording the institution uses** for that kind of request (see "Wording" below).
7. **What to bring back**: which pages to photograph or which screen to save, at what resolution, and where to put
   the file so the agent can read it.

## Wording

- **НИАБ Гродно**: since 1 February 2025 the archive accepts biographical requests from private persons
  (confirmation of a fact about a named person, with the shelf mark), not genealogical research requests (as published by the archive, 2026). Ask
  for what you need in those terms.
- **ЗАГС**: the registry offices issue a **справка** (certificate of record, form No. 12) as well as a repeat
  свидетельство; the справка showed more fields. The Russian civil register (ЕГР ЗАГС) covers records from 1926 (not
  verified).
  Ask the office which document fits your purpose.
- **Regional archives** can suspend genealogical requests for months, or answer postal requests only for some
  years of the church registers (observed 2026). Other records are read in the reading room or by a person acting
  with power of attorney; check the archive's site.
- **ГА РФ** (State Archive of the Russian Federation) and other archives with access rules for personal files:
  ask the archive which documents it needs from you.
- **Ministry of Internal Affairs information centres (ИЦ МВД)**: two of them accepted requests only through the web
  form for citizens' appeals on their site, not by post or e-mail (2026).
- **Bundesarchiv**: personal requests about soldiers before 1945 go to the Abteilung Deutsches Reich (see
  `germany.md`); Soviet prisoners of war have a bilingual form that can be filled in Russian.
- **Arolsen Archives**: free request form; 5-13 months stated.

## Kinship documents: assemble them before you write

Archives of security services and interior ministries asked about a relative's file may ask for documents that
show the applicant's kinship to that person. Ask the archive what it needs; the agent prepares the chain in
advance, one link per document:

```
applicant (b. <year>) -> father Ivan Primerov -> grandfather Pyotr Primerov -> great-grandfather Ivan Primerov
<- his son (the person asked about)
```

For each link list the document that would show it (birth, marriage, death certificate) and whether the family has
it. Flag every document where the surname is spelled differently (Primerov in one, Primerow in another). If a
link has no document, ask the archive or a lawyer what is possible. Consider what a request to a security-service
archive discloses about you and your relatives.

| Butovo memorial centre | by appointment only |
| printed «Списки населённых мест» and other gazetteers without full text | a person reads the PDF page by page |
| reading room of a regional archive | files on the reading room's computers may not be on the public portal at all; screen photos from an earlier visit can show a fond number the public catalogue does not list. Only the archive can say what it is |
| Family History Center | for FamilySearch films restricted to centres |

A reading-room order form records what was ordered, not what was received. Ask the person who went before you
write "already read".

## Photography and scans

- **Re-shoot home archive pages at about 2,500 px or more** on the long side, in diffuse light, one page per frame. At low
  resolution with oblique light a place of burial on a family chart could not be read.
- **Messenger apps compress photos.** Scans sent through a messenger arrive reduced, and that can become the
  bottleneck of a whole transcription. Ask for the original files, or for the photos to be sent as documents.
- **Some archive viewers do not zoom above 100 %.** Read by screenshot and zoom on the screen.
- Where the reading room allows photography (ask first; some forbid it), and files cannot be
  downloaded, a human takes screen photos; ask for the file title bar to be in the frame, so that the shelf mark is visible.

## Letters to private people

A living person is written to by a living person, briefly, in their own words. The agent prepares the facts and
two or three questions; the researcher writes the message and says plainly that AI helped to prepare it.

What the agent hands over for a letter to a person:

- who the person is and why they might know (their published work, their tree, their forum post), without
  speculation about them;
- the two or three facts the person needs to recognise the family (names in the spelling they use, place, years);
- two or three questions, short, each answerable in one line;
- what not to ask for (anything the person would have to search for on your behalf);
- for the owner of another tree: ask where a fact came from (a document or family memory), and ask for a full-size
  photo rather than taking it through "Confirm Match".

Contacts of private people stay with the researcher; agents do not need them.

Archives are different: their forms are official, and a full, formal request works there.

## Relatives and informants

- **Questionnaires to relatives** work when a person sends them and a person answers. Answers often mention letters
  and photos that were not sent; following up is a human's job.
- **A living informant's word is evidence of kinship in its own right**; weigh it with the documents and record
  what was said, by whom and when.

## Phone calls, visits and cemeteries

Some institutions accept requests only through their own web form, and some places are open by appointment only
(above): the appointment is a human step.

Cemetery registers give less than a visit. The Estonian national cemetery register (`kalmistud.ee`) gives dates
of birth, death and burial and the neighbours in the plot, but no images of the gravestones. Where the stone itself
matters (an inscription that disagrees with a document), a person goes or asks someone local.

## DNA

Taking the sample, consenting to matching, uploading or transferring results and writing to DNA
matches are all human steps. The agent can prepare a list of surnames and places for
the person to check against the match list. See `genealogy-services.md`.

A DNA test also reveals information about relatives who did not take it and can show unexpected parentage. Read
the company's privacy terms (where data are stored, who may access them) before taking a test, and test another person
only with their informed consent.

## Questions an archive alone can answer

Some doubts no amount of searching resolves. Write them down as questions for the archive and hand them over:

- what a fond number seen only in a reading room corresponds to in the public catalogue;
- what a lock or restriction mark on a file means;
- whether a file listed in an old order was actually issued.

## When the agent should not do a step

If the agent cannot or should not do a step on a third-party site, stop and hand it to the researcher.
