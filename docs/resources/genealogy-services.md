# Genealogy services and other people's trees

Commercial and community services (MyHeritage, FamilySearch, Geneanet, Geni, Familio, forums, DNA companies) as
they serve research on families from the former Russian Empire and the USSR. Every behaviour below is an
observation from 2026 with its month, not a guarantee; sites change. The examples are composed illustrations; the
patterns come from real work, the particulars do not.

## The rule that governs this whole file

Someone else's tree is a hypothesis, never a source. The person with the document wins, not the person with the
file. And a tree that looks independent is often your own data coming back to you through third hands.

Before you treat a match or a tree as independent evidence, open its sources tab and read what is there. Typical
findings:

- a Geni profile whose only "source" is `Geni World Family Tree`, that is, Geni itself;
- a private tree on MyHeritage copied from that same Geni tree;
- a first review of MyHeritage matches after uploading your own GEDCOM in which most matches are echoes of your
  own data (Record Matches from `Geni World Family Tree` or from a tree copied from it), a few point to records you
  already hold, and only a couple come from outside.

In one project most first matches were echoes (2026); independent matches were few. Do not confirm a match before reading its source:
confirming pours the other tree's errors back into yours.

Two outside trees that disagree with each other confirm nothing: two trees may give the same woman two different
maiden names and two different places of birth.

A tree is working notes, not testimony. Check the revision history where a service keeps one: a field changed
several times without a source is a note, not a finding.

## Overview

Reach classes: `open page` (a public page reached without a login. An agent reads it only if the site's terms of
use and robots rules allow automated reading; otherwise treat the site as `own browser`), `own browser`
(used by the researcher personally in a browser: it needs a login or a subscription, or it does not serve
automated requests), `human only` (payment, contract, a person-to-person contact), `dead`.

| site | what it holds for this region | reach | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| MyHeritage `myheritage.com` | record collections (Russian marriages, Kyiv consistory duplicates, Grodno voters 1912, Polish newspapers, Arolsen, Soviet war casualties, FamilySearch tree), millions of private trees, Smart and Record Matches | own browser, subscription | does not serve automated access |
| FamilySearch `familysearch.org` | church books and revision lists of Belarus and Ukraine on film, indexes, catalogue | own browser, free login | does not serve automated access |
| `nashipredki.com` | a catalogue of East European archive films, compiled from FamilySearch data | open page | on the fonds we checked (2026) it listed fewer films than FamilySearch; a zero there is not a zero at FamilySearch |

| Familio `familio.org` | Russian-language tree service; GEDCOM export; a searchable 1906 provincial place list | own browser, account | export observations below |
| ВГД forum `forum.vgd.ru` | volunteer transcriptions, archive inventories, local experts | open page | some pages hidden from guests |
| FamilyTreeDNA `familytreedna.com` | DNA projects by region (Belarus, Grodno and others) | own browser | group pages readable anonymously |

## How these services are used

All the logged-in and subscription services above are used by the researcher personally, in their own browser, as
a normal user. They do not serve automated access, and an agent does not log in to them, drive them or read them on
the researcher's behalf. What an agent does is prepare the search (names in every spelling, places with their
jurisdiction for the year, the question each search should answer) and afterwards work from the notes the
researcher takes.

Confirming or rejecting a match, saving a record to the tree or a photo from someone else's tree are edits of the
researcher's tree. The researcher decides and makes them, one at a time, with the site's own buttons.

## MyHeritage

Subscription service; the researcher's own browser only.

### How search behaves (as a user sees it)

- Use exact search for the surname. A loose surname search returned millions of loosely related records.
- Latin and Cyrillic spellings return different sets. Search both (Primerov and Примеров).
- Extra criteria rank, they do not filter: a control place far from the family changed the order of results, not
  their number.
- Each collection has its own search form with an event-place field. That field ranks too: a stricter place
  setting can leave the count unchanged while later pages drift to another town. Count the records up to the last
  page where the place is still the one you asked for, not by the total.
- A record seen from the father's side is not a second document. A person who exists only in the "father" field
  has no card and no fields of their own.
- In the Kyiv consistory duplicates collection the transcription gave one surname in several different forms (one
  with a `?` inside, observed September 2026). Collect a branch by given name or patronymic together with the
  village; parents in a record are searchable as persons. A village name also catches its namesakes in other
  uezds; sort them out by church. Take the archive reference (volume, page, entry, film, image) from the record
  card, not from the result list.

### Matches: Smart Matches and Record Matches

- Matches exist only after you upload your own tree. Before uploading, remove living people or reduce them to
  what they agreed to (see `checking-privacy-before-sharing`).
- Matches seen on Geni do not carry over: matches expected from a Geni tree turned out to be Geni's own, and on
  MyHeritage that tree had none. A fresh GEDCOM may still be indexing; check again after a few days.
- Record matches to collections you transcribed yourself are echoes of your own data. With two of your trees on
  the site, every record is shown twice.
- Decide about re-uploading the GEDCOM before confirming anything. A new upload binds the matches to the new tree,
  and confirmations made on the old one are lost.
- Persons taken only from someone else's tree should be left out of the next GEDCOM you upload (mark them, for
  example with an id prefix, and filter on export). Otherwise they come back as matches that look like
  confirmation.
- A match card shows the other tree's photo only as a small thumbnail. The full-size file is offered only through
  "Confirm Match", which edits your tree; otherwise ask the tree's owner (see `needs-a-human.md`).
- An implausible family in a match (a second parent of the same sex, an impossible age) is a dispute to record,
  not a link to add.

## FamilySearch

A free login is required; the researcher uses it in their own browser. Some films are marked as viewable only in a
Family History Center (a human visit, see `needs-a-human.md`). A page seen without a login is never a zero.

- **Fuzzy ranking.** With no exact hit the engine still returns unrelated surnames with a low relevance score: a
  full result list is not a list of hits.
- **Collections.** "Belarus, Grodno, Church Books, 1649-1915": about 1.48 million records,
  4.06 million persons and 441,000 images (August 2026). Belarusian and Ukrainian church books and revision lists
  from the Ukrainian central historical archive and oblast archives are on film; the Pskov Lutheran registers copied
  for the St Petersburg consistory are on film and indexed.
- **Images by place.** The "Images" search lists the film groups for one place and the items on each film with
  years and the archive reference of each item.
- **Open the first image of a group in single view, not in the grid of thumbnails**, which can hang the tab, and
  move through the film with the "Image" number field.
- **The order of images on a film need not match the sum of the group sizes**: on one film an image already
  belonged to the next parish's book although the counts said otherwise.
- **Place labels can come from a table of contents.** A confession list labelled with a village held only the
  churches of the uezd town.
- **An index zero is not an image zero.** A birth missing from the index of a regional church-book collection can
  be explained by the images: the parish book on the film may begin late in the year, with earlier births only in
  the consistory duplicates, which the index does not cover. The full-text search did not cover these books at all.
  State a negative result as "not in the index of collection X", and read the images before you write "not in the
  book".
- **Search under the street name too.** In Ukrainian villages a man could be registered under a surname formed
  from his grandfather's name.
- **What is digitised.** `nashipredki.com` is a catalogue of East European archive films, compiled from
  FamilySearch data; on the fonds we checked (2026) it listed fewer films than FamilySearch, so a film present at
  FamilySearch can be missing there. A fond page there does not mean the fond is digitised.
- **Geneanet and MyHeritage carry FamilySearch index collections.** A hit in both is one source seen twice.

## Geneanet

The researcher's own browser with a Geneanet account; record cards need Premium.

- Prove the search works with a common surname before trusting a zero.
- The search form does not accept Cyrillic. Search the Latin and German forms (Primerow, Przymierow).
- What it holds for this region: FamilySearch index collections "Russia, Select Marriages / Births and Baptisms /
  Deaths and Burials" (Lutheran consistory copies; the place is written as the German name of the town), "Latvia
  BMD 1854-1909" and "Latvia Census 1854-1897" (the Latvian archive's images made searchable by name), "Estonia,
  Census, Tax and House Lists" (Estonian revision lists); an index of the Estonian press; private trees.
- Without Premium a result row shows name, spouse or parents, record type and a 25-year date interval. The record
  card (dates, parents, source) is Premium.
- Latvia BMD cards carry no parish: only names, dates and parents. Recognise a family by the pair of parents and
  the maiden surname. The census cards carry the place but not the census year.
- "Hints" compares only Latin-script names; in September 2026 it returned zero against a tree with an obvious
  overlap. "Search for Relatives" needs a deceased root person.
- For Russian and Ukrainian families searches in September and October 2026 found only namesakes; the value was in
  the Baltic and Lutheran index collections.

## Geni

The researcher's own login (a free Basic plan is enough to read).

- **Geni shows a revision history with previous field values (observed 2026).** We did not find an equivalent
  view in the other services we used. Open a profile, then the "history" link next to "About"; the "Facts" tab shows a line by
  line diff.
- **The "Restore" buttons in the history edit the tree.** Do not press them.
- Check the sources tab of every profile: `Sources 1 -> One saved record -> Geni World Family Tree` means the
  profile cites itself.

## Familio

A Russian-language tree service; Familio is a tree service, not a record collection. Observations from the one
export we examined (August-September 2026); check your own export for each:

- **In the one export we examined, the original typed place sat in `2 NOTE`, a sibling of `2 PLAC`.** In that
  export, typed places had been replaced by entries of the service's place catalogue, which in many events did not
  match the typed place: several distinct places (manors, a parish, a cemetery) appeared as one village far away,
  and the farms of one parish as one nearby town. Most could be recovered from `NOTE`.
- **In that export, `3 NOTE` under `PLAC` held a service URL** (`familio.org/settlements/...`), not the original text.
- **In that export, `2 NOTE` was not always a place.** Some events held a comment there. A blind replacement of `PLAC` by `NOTE`
  breaks the graph.
- In that tree, **`MAP` coordinates** of persons born before the Second World War pointed to present-day places;
  check them.
- **In that export, `2 CAUS` was more often a place than a cause of death**, but not always: in the form "cause | place" the left
  part was a real cause. A filter that dropped the whole field deleted real causes of death.
- **In the tree we imported, the dates were registration dates, not event dates** (checked on several certificates),
  and some fatherhood links had no document behind them. Check both before importing.
- **In that export, the Julian calendar escape came after the qualifier**: `2 DATE ABT @#DJULIAN@ 1840`. An importer that strips
  `@#DJULIAN@` only at the start of the line silently lost some old-style marks. General rule: when parsing someone
  else's GEDCOM, do not assume an optional element stands where your own exporter puts it.
- Familio also hosts a searchable version of the 1906 place list of Vitebsk province (see
  `libraries-and-reference.md`).

## GEDCOM import and export

- **Date qualifiers.** If your own notes write "about", "before", "after" or "between" in another language,
  translate them to GEDCOM on export: `ABT 1840`, `BEF 1815`, `AFT 1925`, `BET 1781 AND 1784`. A dual year
  `1760/61` is legal GEDCOM.
- **Check an exporter by reading its code for every form you intend to write, not by a run on today's data.** An
  export can pass its check only because the forms "before" and "after" do not yet exist in the graph, and fail on
  `2 DATE 15 MAY after 1925` once they do.
- **Old style.** If your exporter writes no `@#DJULIAN@` (check: zero occurrences of `@#D` in the file), MyHeritage
  receives the calendar only as a human-readable note.
- **Re-uploading** to MyHeritage re-binds all matches to the new tree (see "Matches" above).

## Compare trees by `_UID`, never by name

When you merge an imported GEDCOM into your own records, match persons by the exporter's stable id (`_UID` or the
service's own id), never by name. With matching by name, several men of the same given name and surname in one
parish produce imaginary merge conflicts and false facts, which can survive several rounds of checking and raise
questions about anomalies that do not exist.

A name, and even a name with a year, is not an identity: namesakes in one parish are the rule. An archive file
under the right surname can belong to another family; the volost decides. The surname is not an identifier.

## Forums

### ВГД forum (`forum.vgd.ru`, "All-Russian Genealogical Tree")

- Readable without login; pages are in the `cp1251` encoding.
- A topic link taken from a search engine may carry the wrong section. Open the topic and page through it with its
  own page links.
- Some pages of some topics are not shown to guests. At times the whole forum answers "service temporarily
  unavailable"; that is not a zero.
- Check forum reference posts against a gazetteer.
- Forum members often hold transcriptions or offer to look up villages on request. That is a contact between
  people (see `needs-a-human.md`), not a data source.
- A page summary from a fetch tool for a `cp1251` page reported two wrong postal addresses of an archive. Read the
  page itself.

### Other forums

- `sgvavia.ru` (aviation history forum): guest search works.
- `moypolk.ru` ("My regiment" portal): open page.

## DNA services

| service | what was seen (2026) |
|---|---|
| FamilyTreeDNA `familytreedna.com` | regional projects (for example Belarus and Grodno) with public pages; member surname lists are drawn in the browser |

Taking a test, consenting to matching and contacting matches are human steps (see `needs-a-human.md`).
A DNA test also reveals information about relatives who did not take it and can show unexpected parentage. Read
the company's privacy terms (where data are stored, who may access them) before taking a test, and test another person
only with their informed consent.
