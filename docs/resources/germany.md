# Germany and occupied Europe: prisoner-of-war and forced-labour records

Resources for Soviet prisoners of war (POWs), forced labourers (Ostarbeiter) and displaced persons of 1941-1945.
Statements about site behaviour are observations with their month. The examples are composed illustrations; the
patterns come from real work, the particulars do not. Reach classes: **open page** (a public page reached without a login. An agent reads it only if the site's terms of use and robots rules allow automated reading; otherwise treat the site as `own browser`), **own browser** (used by the researcher personally in a browser:
login, subscription, or no automated requests served), **human only** (written request, signature), **dead**; see
[README.md](README.md).

## Which archive holds what

| institution / site | what it holds | reach | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arolsen Archives online collections | concentration camps, Gestapo, civilian forced labour, displaced persons (DP) | open page (search) | POW personal cards (Personalkarte I) are **not** indexed; a timeout is not a zero |
| Arolsen Archives, written request | the same holdings searched by staff | human only (free web form) | 5-13 months stated |
| Bundesarchiv, Abteilung Deutsches Reich | personal records of military origin before 1945, including the database of Soviet POWs held in camps and work detachments inside the Reich | human only (written application) | |
| Saxon Memorials Foundation `ru.stsg.de` | Soviet POWs in camps in Saxony (Zeithain and others), with dates and graves | open page (search) | Cyrillic and Latin |
| `poisk.re`, search of captured POW cards | captured German POW cards kept in the Russian Ministry of Defence archive: stalag, POW number, date and place of capture, archive reference | open page | long result lists are truncated: narrow the query |
| Norwegian Digital Archive `digitalarkivet.no` | Soviet POWs in Norway (Organisation Todt lists, repatriation office, labour cards) | open page (catalogue and scans) | scans can only be browsed, not searched by name |

## Arolsen Archives

### Online collections

- The online search answered without login (September 2026). It timed out on some days and answered again later:
  a timeout is an episode, not a rule, and not a zero.
- **Control before trusting a zero**: a common Russian surname in German transliteration (`Iwanow`) should return
  its usual large count. If the control does not, the service is not working.
- **The wildcard `*` works** and also returns phonetic neighbours, including spellings with C for K and doubled
  consonants. A mask is cheaper than typing spelling variants by hand.
- A wildcard and a place filter (e.g. Riga, Latvia, Livland) can be combined in one query.
- For a common surname, cards without a birth date and with the same given name cannot be told apart from the
  online card.
- Many transliterations of one surname plus a mask can give zero: a real zero for the online index, not for the
  archive.

### What the online index does not cover

- The index covers concentration camps, Gestapo, civilian forced labour and DPs. Arolsen itself says (Russian page
  "Ваш запрос в архив") that it has almost no documents on POWs, including POWs who were also made to work.
- A Soviet soldier who was only a POW is therefore very unlikely to be found there. Search the Bundesarchiv POW
  database and the Russian-held captured cards (poisk.re) instead.

### Written request (human only)

- Free web form on the Arolsen site, available in Russian; the stated turnaround is 5-13 months; the form asked
  for no kinship documents (September 2026; check the archive's site).
- A negative reply can come quickly as a standard letter; it closes the question for that archive. Do not write
  again about the same person.

## Bundesarchiv (German Federal Archives)

- Personal requests about soldiers before 1945 go to the **Abteilung Deutsches Reich** (contact details: see the
  archive's own site). Earlier addresses and forms are out of date.
- For Soviet POWs, the page "Запросы о советских военнопленных" (requests about Soviet POWs) under the section on
  personal records of military origin before 1945 offers:
  - a bilingual fillable PDF request form that can be filled in Russian;
  - the user application, to be signed;
  - a Russian translation of the user application, for understanding only.
- The database covers camps and work detachments **on the territory of the Reich**.
- An automatic acknowledgement arrives by e-mail; do not send the request again after it.

## Saxon Memorials (Stiftung Sächsische Gedenkstätten)

- The Russian-language search page of `ru.stsg.de` searches by surname or its first letters; Cyrillic and Latin both
  work. The Zeithain camp has its own page on the site (November 2026).

## Captured POW cards on poisk.re

- Its search of captured POW cards returns the captured German POW cards: stalag, POW number, date and place of capture,
  Russian archive reference.
- Place and year cannot be added to the query. For a common surname search "surname + rare patronymic".
- A full name of three words can give a false zero where two words find the person (September 2026): search by
  two words and read the patronymic in the results.
- A "too many requests" page (429) is a refusal: stop.
- Long result lists are truncated: narrow the query.

## Norway

- `digitalarkivet.no` shows its catalogue without login. The Soviet POW sources ("Soviet prisoners of war,
  alphabetically A-Z" of Organisation Todt, the repatriation office, the Organisation Todt labour card index) are
  marked "Browse scans / Not searchable": a name is found only by reading the scans.

## What ГА РФ holds on captivity and repatriation

From the archive's published guides (2026):

- The State Archive of the Russian Federation (ГА РФ) holds repatriation lists (fond R-9526) and
  lists of the Extraordinary State Commission, but **not** the filtration and check files on returned POWs: those
  are with the regional FSB directorate or the state archive of the region the person returned to.

## Lessons

- An online index zero is only a zero for that index. The written request (Arolsen, Bundesarchiv) is the way to
  revise it, once per person.
- Prepare every transliteration (Russian to German: `-ow`, `-ew`, `-sch-`, `-tsch-`; Estonian and Latvian forms), and
  use the wildcard where the site supports it.
- A card without a birth date cannot identify a person with a common name; record such cards as unresolved, not as
  matches or non-matches.
