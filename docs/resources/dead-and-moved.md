# Dead, moved and repurposed domains

Domains that a search engine, an old forum post or a language model will keep suggesting, and that waste time
because they do not answer, answer with something else, or never existed. Observed August to November 2026; a
domain can change owner or come back at any time. Domains are listed because they are public facts; the archives
and projects behind them are often alive under another address. The examples are composed illustrations; the
patterns come from real work, the particulars do not.

## The rule: do not open, do not cite

A domain that no longer shows the institution's content needs to be opened by neither an agent nor a human, and
any link to it in your notes, reports or a book should be removed. If the institution behind it is
alive, cite the institution and its current address; if it has no site, the route is a letter.

Classify every dead address before you give up on it:

| kind | meaning | what to do |
|---|---|---|
| technical | the domain is dead, the documents are not | web archive, a successor site, or a letter |
| final | the address never existed or is a typo | stop; use the correct address |
| conditional | alive but empty or broken now; may change | note the condition that would reopen it |

A domain that does not resolve is not a resource that never existed: `lgdb.lv` and `deportacija.lv` below are
technical losses.

## How to recognise a domain that changed hands

- A redirect from the institution's old domain to a domain that has nothing to do with the institution.
- The page title and content no longer belong to the institution.
- The institution's other pages (its parent body, a regional portal, Wikipedia, recent archive guides) give a
  different current address.
- The web archive shows a normal institutional site up to some year and different content after it.

Do not follow such a redirect, and do not paste the target into notes.

## Stale advice

- «Бессмертный барак» ("Immortal Barrack") does not move from `.com` to `.ru`; it is the reverse: `.ru` redirects
  to `.com`.
- A Ukrainian oblast archive domain that stopped resolving had no working alternative; printed inventories of
  such archives are published on `uk.wikisource.org`.

## Two more ways to be misled about a site

- **A search engine or a fetch tool can hallucinate.** A web search read through a fetch tool returned ten results
  about an unrelated software product for a rare surname. Language models produce plausible domains (`saaga.ee`,
  `geni.ee`) with full confidence.
- **A fetch tool's summary is not the page.** A summary of a `cp1251` page reported two wrong postal addresses of
  an archive. Take addresses, shelf marks and numbers from the page itself.

## Recovering a dead site

The Wayback Machine has a documented public API:

1. `archive.org/wayback/available?url=<url>` gives the nearest snapshot.
2. `web.archive.org/web/<year>/<url>` opens it.
3. `web.archive.org/cdx/search/cdx?url=<prefix>*` lists the captured pages under a path.
4. Record in your notes the date of the snapshot you cited.
